How I work

Data first, then the field

I start with what the numbers show, then go and watch the work: Mixpanel funnels and user calls at Kirana Club, shopfloor walkthroughs and engineer interviews at Wootz.Work.

Assist, don't automate blindly

Where an error flows into costing or quality, AI drafts and flags what it is unsure about, and a person confirms. Speed comes from faster review, not blind trust.

Build it when that's faster

I prototype and integrate with APIs, low-code platforms and code, so ideas get tested inside real workflows quickly instead of waiting in a backlog.

AI-assisted extraction from engineering drawings

Wootz.Work

Problem

Engineers copied part numbers, materials and quantities from drawing PDFs by hand: ~20–25 minutes per drawing, and details still got missed.

What I built

Assisted extraction with OCR and an LLM fallback, confidence-coloured fields for quick review, and placeholders that flag missing drawings early.

Outcome

~5 minutes per drawing, about 5× less manual processing.

Read the case study
Context

In manufacturing, the bill-of-materials (BOM) table inside a drawing is the source of truth for costing, planning and procurement. Assemblies often have 20–30 child parts, and drawings missing from the client side usually surfaced late.

The insight

Watching engineers work, the errors came from fatigue while scanning dense tables, not from typing. They didn't need raw extraction; they needed fast, reliable validation.

Key decision: assist, don't automate

A wrong value here flows straight into costing and supplier communication, so a fully automatic extractor was too risky. I designed an assisted system: OCR does the extraction, an LLM handles contextual edge cases as a fallback, and any field the system is unsure about is colour-coded for a quick human check.

What shipped
  • A table interface where users drop screenshots of BOM columns and get structured rows back.
  • Auto-tagging of child parts and BOM rows to validate structure.
  • Confidence-based colouring so reviewers only look closely where it matters.
  • Placeholder documents for missing drawings, so gaps are visible early and trigger client follow-ups.
  • Direct integration with the engineering platform, so extracted data feeds the assembly tree and downstream workflows.
My role

Owned the technical design and implementation end to end. I co-own the wider engineering-drawing platform, where I also designed a tree view that mirrors assembly hierarchy and a data-archive pipeline that keeps the low-code platform fast as data grows.

Impact

Processing time fell from ~20–25 minutes to ~5 minutes per drawing, with fewer rechecks and earlier detection of missing drawings.

Measured through user feedback and observed workflow; this internal tool had no formal analytics.

Stack

Paddle OCR, Google Document AI, GPT · HTML, CSS, JavaScript · Render · Glide APIs, Cloudinary

RFQ Assistant: structuring messy client requirements

Wootz.Work

Problem

Client requests arrive as short, incomplete descriptions. Engineers spent ~2–3 hours inferring missing details and structuring them for suppliers.

What I built

An LLM assistant that turns raw descriptions into an editable table, with the reasoning behind each assumption and a confidence tag per field.

Outcome

First-pass structuring down to ~10–20 minutes of review in early use.

Read the case study
Context

Before suppliers can quote, someone has to turn a client's shorthand (product, material, standard, finish) into a complete, supplier-ready requirement. Quality depended on experience: newer engineers often had to ask seniors for help, and similar requests kept recurring with nothing reusable stored.

The insight

Most of the time went into thinking through ambiguities, not typing. That made it a pattern-recognition and reasoning problem, which suits an LLM, as long as a person stays in control of the assumptions.

What shipped
  • Paste raw text or Excel, choose which fields to extract, and add context such as language or internal codes.
  • Output as a spreadsheet-like table with standard fields plus custom ones, an assumption-reason column, and colour-coded confidence for every value.
  • Every cell is editable, and the final table exports as a supplier-ready Excel file.
My role

Owned it end to end: problem framing, UX, data schema and confidence model, prompt design and evaluation, frontend build and deployment.

Impact

First-pass structuring dropped from ~2–3 hours to ~10–20 minutes of review, with more consistent and complete supplier requests and less dependence on senior engineers.

What's next

Improving how user context is captured, so the assistant helps newer engineers as much as experienced ones.

Stack

React, Handsontable, Tailwind CSS · OpenAI API with structured prompting

Request-to-quote pipeline

Wootz.Work

Problem

Sales worked outside the internal request platform. Requests and clarifications moved over email and WhatsApp, so context got lost and the right contact was often unclear.

What I built

A pipeline connecting sales and supply teams: automated request capture, document workspace creation, team routing and templated quote generation.

Outcome

~350 requests and 150+ quotes processed since launch.

Read the case study
The design problem

Sales and internal teams needed to work in one system without seeing the same things. Sales needed status, owner and a place to raise questions; internal costing and supplier discussions had to stay restricted.

What shipped
  • Incoming client emails become requests automatically; on approval, a document workspace is created and files are uploaded, so links stay stable and handoffs are clean.
  • Role-based views and conditional visibility, so each role sees its own view of the same data.
  • Structured query threads with in-app and email notifications, replacing scattered chats.
  • Templated quote generation at the end of the flow.
My role

Led the build: translated the requirements into access logic, notification rules and UX, restructured parts of the data model for multi-role access, and integrated it into the live platform without breaking existing internal workflows.

Stack

Glide (role-based access, conditional notifications) · email automation · cloud document storage integration

Supplier intelligence platform

Wootz.Work

Problem

Finding the right supplier depended on memory, calls and WhatsApp: ~20–30 minutes per search, and existing suppliers were sometimes contacted as if they were new.

What I built

A platform for supplier onboarding, NDAs, capability mapping and assessments, so sourcing starts with a search instead of a phone call.

Outcome

1,600+ suppliers structured; discovery down to ~2–3 minutes (team-reported).

Read the case study
The insight

After talking with sourcing, engineering and operations, it was clear the knowledge already existed. It just wasn't structured or retrievable: people remembered why they used a supplier once, not everything that supplier could do.

What shipped
  • Onboarding and documentation, with NDA PDFs and emails generated automatically.
  • Capability mapping across fabrication, hardware and consumables, and system integration.
  • Assessments, review history and flags with reasons, so reliability is visible at a glance.
Key decision

I compared Glide and AppSheet and chose Google Sheets with AppSheet for faster iteration, stronger automation and lower operational friction.

My role

Owned it end to end: user research, workflow and system design, platform choice, implementation, rollout demos and iteration.

What I learned

A companion browser extension for saving supplier research (my idea from discovery, built by the team) excited new hires but saw low adoption from existing staff, because of retrieval UX and privacy concerns. Saving people time isn't enough if they don't trust where their work goes.

Discovery time is based on team feedback rather than instrumented analytics.

Quality & inspection system

Wootz.Work

Problem

Quality doubts on the shopfloor were handled over calls and chats, so fixes weren't traceable. Inspections with 25–30 points per assembly relied on memory or marked-up paper drawings.

What I built

A mobile-first quality platform with issue threads and CAPA workflows, and an inspection system: plan inspection maps on drawings, then execute them step by step on mobile.

Outcome

Inspection cycle time cut by 50%+; 600+ inspections across 150+ projects by 50+ users.

Read the case study
The insight

Engineers had the expertise. What was missing was a structured place for quality discussions and corrective actions, and a way to plan inspections before doing them.

Quality platform
  • Engineers raise issues immediately as structured threads and escalate them into CAPA (corrective and preventive action) workflows.
  • Critical control points, drawings and project context are available in one place, and project journeys export as PDFs for audits.
  • Chosen for mobile use on shopfloors with patchy connectivity, with daily email reports and notifications.

My role: co-built the first version with my manager, then owned the platform: CAPA workflows, integration with the engineering platform, notifications, reporting and maintenance.

Inspection system
  • Plan once on the drawing: mark points, instructions and tools, with stage-wise maps and no duplicate drawing copies.
  • On mobile, the drawing zooms to each point, shows the expected value and records the measurement.
  • Each inspection produces a structured report with screenshots and values.

My role: led discovery, UX, architecture and integration; a junior engineer implemented it while I owned testing, rollout and delivery.

What I learned

Adoption was strongest in the quality team. For small assemblies, project engineers saw a full inspection map as overhead. Next, I would segment the flow by assembly complexity and make it lighter for fast-moving engineers.

Building for Bharat users: KYC, incentives and profiling

Kirana Club (acquired by Meesho)

Problem

High KYC rejection, an incentive section new users didn't understand, and reward costs rising with growth.

What I did

Redesigned the KYC flow and incentive section, ran an A/B test on the points system, and built a user-profiling pipeline.

Outcome

~10% lower KYC rejection, ~30% faster activation, ~5% lower reward cost.

Read the case study
KYC redesign

Funnel data plus calls with support and users showed that users picked the first option in a document dropdown but uploaded a different document, many lacked the required documents, and personal vs shop KYC was confusing. We switched the dropdown to buttons, added more document options and a "KYC without documents" flow, made rejection reasons specific, and trained support and ops on the new flow. Rejection rates fell ~10% week on week.

Incentive section

New users earned their first points through notifications rather than the incentive section, and many didn't understand it. With design and tech, I reworked its information architecture. Time to first points earned fell ~30%, and new-user engagement rose ~5%.

Reward cost A/B test

Points for being followed were the second-largest source of points for established users but added little value. I tested reducing them for 10% of those users, tracking engagement and support tickets, then rolled out the change: reward cost fell ~5% month on month.

User profiling

With the brands and sales teams, I identified proxy questions that predict a shop's scale, surveyed ~150 engaged users (80% response, 0.6 correlation with manually assessed shop size), and set up a profiling pipeline for 10,000 users to support targeted brand campaigns.

Also managed the release of app version 5.3 and owned KPI dashboards in Mixpanel.
